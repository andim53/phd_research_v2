We present a cheap, reference-anchored global-optimization screening for
amorphous interstitial alloys. The amorphous state is modeled as the
low-energy basin around a chosen crystalline reference and enumerated with a
surrogate-guided search; amorphization is confirmed structurally with the
Steinhardt $q_6$ crystalline fraction, cross-checked against simulated X-ray
diffraction. Applied to interstitial Pt(P), the screening locates the onset
of structural amorphization at 20--30~at.\% P: the crystalline fraction
collapses from 0.99 in the undoped host to 0.09--0.18, the effect saturates
with further loading, and cell expansion mildly promotes disorder. The
predicted onset is consistent with the experimental amorphization of
ion-implanted amorphous Pt(P), whose onset maps to $\sim$36~at.\% P by a
box-model dose conversion, both in the $\sim$20--40~at.\% range. The
screening samples the amorphous basin at a fraction of the cost of
first-principles melt-quench molecular dynamics, offering a cheap,
structurally verified route to the onset of amorphization in interstitial
alloys.
