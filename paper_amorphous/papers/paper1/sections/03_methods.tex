\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Reference-anchored global optimization}\label{sec:methods-search}

We model the amorphous state as the low-energy basin around a chosen
crystalline reference and enumerate that basin with a surrogate-guided
global-optimization search, rather than by melt-quench molecular dynamics
\cite{gofee2020,gofee2022,agox2022}. The reference is a \emph{physical choice}
the user controls: for a host with a single stable phase the reference is
unique, whereas for a polymorphic host it is a genuine choice. Here the fcc
Pt host has a single stable phase, so its reference is unique; we return to
the polymorphic case (Ta, W) in the Supplementary Information
\cite{colin2017,kozhukhovska2026,barmak2017,liubarmak2016,chattaraj2020}.

The search is seeded from the reference topology. Starting from the
DFT-optimized fcc Pt lattice constant $a = 3.975534$~\AA{} and a $3\times3\times3$
supercell (Pt$_{108}$), we add P interstitials to a target atomic fraction
$C$ via $B = C A / (1 - C)$, giving Pt$_{108}$P$_{27}$ (20~at.\% P) and
Pt$_{108}$P$_{46}$ (30~at.\% P). Three supercell families are considered,
differing only in the cell expansion applied to the host: $+0\%$
(11.927~\AA), $+3\%$ (12.284~\AA), and $+5\%$ (12.523~\AA); the $+10\%$
family is excluded from scope. Candidate structures are described by the
Oganov--Valle angular fingerprint \cite{oganov2009}, and a Gaussian-process
regression surrogate is built from a sum of two radial-basis-function kernels
with a repulsive prior \cite{biswas2017,artrith2018}. A
lower-confidence-bound acquisition function ($\kappa = 2$) selects candidates
for density-functional evaluation \cite{gofee2022}.

Three structure generators enter the candidate mix progressively
\cite{agox2022}. The \emph{amorphous randomizer} starts from the reference
P-interstitial alloy and randomly displaces every atom: for each atom it
attempts up to 500 random displacements inside a sphere of radius
1.5~\AA{} (uniform-volume sampling), keeping only displacements that stay
inside the cell and do not place the atom too close to a neighbour. This
seeds the search near the reference topology. The \emph{rattle generator}
displaces a random subset of atoms by up to 1.5~\AA{}, producing small local
perturbations of an existing candidate. The \emph{interstitial-permutation
generator} swaps the positions of atoms of different species (Pt~$\leftrightarrow$~P)
among the interstitial sites, up to 20 swaps with 20 attempts each, and
applies a small rattling displacement (1.5~\AA{}) to the swapped atoms,
redistributing P among sites to explore different interstitial arrangements.

\subsection{Iteration timeline}\label{sec:methods-timeline}

The search runs as an iteration counter $i$. At each iteration the active
phase generates $N$ candidate structures, which are scored by the
Gaussian-process surrogate; the lower-confidence-bound acquisition selects
$M$ of them, and density-functional theory (DFT) evaluates those $M$. The
results are stored in a database and $i$ increments by one. The generator mix
and the relaxation gate switch on at specific iterations, as summarized in
Fig.~\ref{fig:methods-timeline}:

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/methods_timeline.png}
  \caption{Iteration timeline of the surrogate-guided search. The search
      runs as an iteration counter $i$; at each iteration the active phase
      generates $N$ candidates, the Gaussian-process surrogate scores them,
      the lower-confidence-bound acquisition selects $M$, and DFT evaluates
      those $M$, with the results stored in the database and $i$ incrementing
      by one (feedback loop). The generator mix switches on at specific
      iterations: Phase I ($0 \le i < 10$) uses the amorphous randomizer
      alone; Phase II ($10 \le i < 25$) adds the rattle generator and turns
      on relaxation and LCB guidance; Phase III ($i \ge 25$) swaps in the
      permutation generator.}
  \label{fig:methods-timeline}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Phase I} ($0 \le i < 10$): the amorphous randomizer alone
  generates $N = 10$ candidates. This is the exploration /
  surrogate-building phase --- the Gaussian process has no training data yet,
  so candidates are proposed and DFT-scored to populate the surrogate (no
  LCB guidance).
  \item \textbf{Phase II} ($10 \le i < 25$): the rattle generator joins ---
  $N = 5$ from the amorphous randomizer plus $N = 5$ from the rattle
  generator. Relaxation begins at iteration 10 and the search
    becomes LCB-guided ($\kappa = 2$).
  \item \textbf{Phase III} ($25 \le i$): the permutation generator joins ---
  $N = 5$ from the rattle generator plus $N = 5$ from the permutation
  generator; the amorphous randomizer drops out. The search continues under
  LCB ($\kappa = 2$).
\end{itemize}

Structures from iterations below 10 are generated and DFT-scored to populate
the surrogate but are \emph{not} relaxed; relaxation (and the analysis
filter) begins at iteration 10. Only structures from iteration 10 onward are
retained for analysis.

\subsection{Electronic structure}\label{sec:methods-dft}

Density-functional relaxations are performed with the GPAW code
\cite{gpaw2010,ase2017} using a linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals (LCAO)
basis (double-zeta-polarized) and the PBE functional, with a single
$\Gamma$-centered $k$-point, Fermi--Dirac smearing of 0.05~eV, and forces
converged to 0.05 eV/\AA{} \cite{madanchi2024}.
\subsection{Computational cost}\label{sec:methods-cost}

The surrogate-guided search enumerates the amorphous Pt(P) candidate
ensemble at a fraction of the cost of conventional first-principles
melt-quench molecular dynamics \cite{meltquench2026,artrith2018,nahas2016,activemlp2020}.
Approximately $5.8\times10^{3}$ DFT relaxations across the six Pt(P) leaves
($\sim$2--3$\times10^{2}$ per seed) produce $\sim$5.2$\times10^{3}$ retained
candidate structures, stored in a compact, indexable database ($\sim$8--12~MB
per leaf). This is the same efficiency regime as evolutionary and
machine-learning-assisted amorphous sampling in other systems
($\sim$10$^{3}$ first-principles calculations), and far below the tens of
thousands of reference calculations typically needed to train a general
machine-learned potential for melt-quench.

\subsection{Exploration-to-exploitation transition}\label{sec:methods-explore}

The search transitions sharply from a surrogate-building (exploration) phase
to LCB-guided exploitation at iteration 10. Across 61 seeds and 13 leaves,
the mean DFT energy of retained structures drops sharply at the iteration-10
LCB switch-on, and the drop is universal across every seed. The per-leaf
median drop (exploitation mean minus exploration mean) grows with P loading:
0P $-$156 to $-$218~eV, 20P $-$256 to $-$306~eV, 30P $-$320 to $-$356~eV. For
the representative 20~at.\% P leaf at $+0\%$ cell expansion the mean drops from
$-$375 to $-$390~eV (exploration) to $-$681 to $-$689~eV (exploitation) across
its four seeds. The acquisition value correlates strongly with the DFT energy
for P-doped leaves ($|r| \approx 0.7$--0.9 across all 20P/30P seeds; the
correlation is weak for 0P, $|r| \approx 0.2$--0.7), confirming the surrogate
selects low-energy candidates rather than noise. The per-leaf global minimum
is typically reached in the latter half of the 100-iteration budget (P-leaf
medians 80--99; range 10--100) \cite{gofee2022}.

\subsection{Surrogate predictive performance}\label{sec:methods-surrogate}

The GPR surrogate predicts the DFT energy accurately enough to drive the
search, and its error grows with distance from the minimum and with P
loading. Over the retained ensemble (iteration $\ge$ 10, all nine leaves,
per-leaf relative energy 0--0.3~eV/atom), the mean absolute error (MAE) in
the 0--0.1~eV/atom window is 0.009--0.015 (0P), 0.029--0.034 (20P), and
0.048--0.061 (30P)~eV/atom across the three cells; the GPR uncertainty
$\sigma$ in the same window is 0.019--0.023 (0P), 0.028--0.032 (20P), and
0.043--0.048 (30P)~eV/atom. Both MAE and $\sigma$ rise with distance from the
minimum and with P loading, and the predicted uncertainty tracks the actual
error --- validating the LCB acquisition ($\kappa = 2$). Per-window $R^2$ is
not reported: within 0.1~eV/atom windows the energy variance is tiny relative
to the error, so $R^2$ is negative and uninformative; MAE and GPR $\sigma$
are the meaningful metrics. We note that the DFT ground truth for P leaves is
on under-relaxed geometries (single-DFT-step evaluator), so the measured
error includes that noise and is an upper bound on the true model error
\cite{gofee2022}.

\subsection{Crystallinity verification}\label{sec:methods-crystallinity}

Amorphization is quantified by two complementary metrics --- XRD
crystallinity indices and a per-atom Steinhardt bond-orientational order
parameter --- cross-checked against each other \cite{steinhardt1983}.

XRD patterns are simulated with a powder-diffraction calculator (Cu K$\alpha$,
$2\theta$ 10--90$^{\circ}$, Gaussian broadening $\sigma = 0.15^{\circ}$),
reporting peak-fraction and integrated crystallinity indices. Structures are
binned into relative-energy windows (0.1~eV/atom wide, up to 0.5~eV/atom,
relative to the leaf's global minimum) and a deterministic sample of each
window is averaged \cite{pymatgen2013,ase2017}.

Steinhardt bond-orientational order parameters $q_4$ and $q_6$ are computed
from the first-neighbour shell (cutoff $1.3\times$ the nearest-neighbour
distance); an atom is classified fcc-like when $q_6 > 0.5$ (fcc $q_6 \approx
0.5745$). The crystalline fraction is the mean over retained structures
within 0.3~eV/atom of the leaf's global minimum (relative-energy cap,
per-leaf reference). Partial radial distribution functions $g_{\mathrm{Pt-P}}(r)$
and $g_{\mathrm{P-P}}(r)$ are computed out to 5.0~\AA{} (200 bins)
\cite{ase2017}.
