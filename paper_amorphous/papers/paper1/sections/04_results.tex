\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Computational cost of the screening}\label{sec:results-cost}

The surrogate-guided search enumerates the amorphous Pt(P) candidate
ensemble at a fraction of the cost of conventional first-principles
melt-quench molecular dynamics \cite{meltquench2026,artrith2018,nahas2016,activemlp2020}.
Approximately $5.8\times10^{3}$ DFT relaxations across the six Pt(P) leaves
($\sim$2--3$\times10^{2}$ per seed) produce $\sim$5.2$\times10^{3}$ retained
candidate structures, stored in a compact, indexable database ($\sim$8--12~MB
per leaf). This is the same efficiency regime as evolutionary and
machine-learning-assisted amorphous sampling in other systems
($\sim$10$^{3}$ first-principles calculations), and far below the tens of
thousands of reference calculations typically needed to train a general
machine-learned potential for melt-quench.

\subsection{Exploration-to-exploitation transition}\label{sec:results-explore}

The search transitions sharply from a surrogate-building (exploration) phase
to LCB-guided exploitation at iteration 10. Across 61 seeds and 13 leaves,
the mean DFT energy of retained structures drops sharply at the iteration-10
LCB switch-on, and the drop is universal across every seed. The per-leaf
median drop (exploitation mean minus exploration mean) grows with P loading:
0P $-$156 to $-$218~eV, 20P $-$256 to $-$306~eV, 30P $-$320 to $-$356~eV. For
the representative 20~at.\% P leaf at $+0\%$ cell expansion the mean drops from
$-$375 to $-$390~eV (exploration) to $-$681 to $-$689~eV (exploitation) across
its four seeds. The acquisition value correlates strongly with the DFT energy
for P-doped leaves ($|r| \approx 0.7$--0.9 across all 20P/30P seeds; the
correlation is weak for 0P, $|r| \approx 0.2$--0.7), confirming the surrogate
selects low-energy candidates rather than noise. The per-leaf global minimum
is typically reached in the latter half of the 100-iteration budget (P-leaf
medians 80--99; range 10--100) \cite{gofee2022}.

\subsection{Surrogate predictive performance}\label{sec:results-surrogate}

The GPR surrogate predicts the DFT energy accurately enough to drive the
search, and its error grows with distance from the minimum and with P
loading. Over the retained ensemble (all nine leaves), the mean absolute
error (MAE) in
the 0--0.1~eV/atom window is 0.009--0.015 (0P), 0.029--0.034 (20P), and
0.048--0.061 (30P)~eV/atom across the three cells; the GPR uncertainty
$\sigma$ in the same window is 0.019--0.023 (0P), 0.028--0.032 (20P), and
0.043--0.048 (30P)~eV/atom. Both MAE and $\sigma$ rise with distance from the
minimum and with P loading, and the predicted uncertainty tracks the actual
error --- validating the LCB acquisition. Per-window $R^2$ is
not reported: within 0.1~eV/atom windows the energy variance is tiny relative
to the error, so $R^2$ is negative and uninformative; MAE and GPR $\sigma$
are the meaningful metrics. We note that the DFT ground truth for P leaves is
on under-relaxed geometries (single-DFT-step evaluator), so the measured
error includes that noise and is an upper bound on the true model error
\cite{gofee2022}.

\subsection{Crystallinity: amorphization onset and cell dependence}\label{sec:results-crystallinity}

The crystalline fraction (fraction of atoms with $q_6 > 0.5$)
quantifies how much fcc order survives in each leaf. Adding P interstitials
collapses it: the 0P leaves retain a crystalline fraction of 0.87--0.97,
whereas the 20P and 30P leaves fall to 0.11--0.20 across all three cell
families (Fig.~\ref{fig:crystallinity}). This marks 20--30~at.\% P as the
onset of structural amorphization by the interstitial global-optimization
route. The collapse is consistent with the confusion principle
\cite{greer1993}: each added species frustrates the fcc packing of the host,
favouring glass formation. The XRD integrated crystallinity index
cross-checks the same trend, dropping from 0.81--0.83 to 0.35--0.38 with P
loading.

The amorphizing effect saturates with concentration: the 20P and 30P
crystalline fractions overlap within scatter at every cell ($+0\%$: 0.188 vs
0.200; $+3\%$: 0.143 vs 0.112; $+5\%$: 0.126 vs 0.123), so once P is loaded
the effect does not grow further with loading.

Cell expansion mildly promotes disorder. At fixed composition, the
equilibrium $+0\%$ cell retains the highest crystalline fraction, and $+3\%$
and $+5\%$ expansion reduce it consistently for both 20P and 30P (20P:
0.188 $\to$ 0.143 $\to$ 0.126; 30P: 0.200 $\to$ 0.112 $\to$ 0.123). The
effect is mild --- a promotion of disorder, not a strong amorphization switch
--- and matches the XRD integrated-CI direction, which is weak ($\sim$7\% on
the XRD metric versus $\sim$20--30\% on the per-atom fraction). The mechanism
is free volume: expanding the supercell at fixed occupancy adds free volume,
which sustains the disordered rearrangements that characterise the amorphous
state \cite{yang2025}.

The disordering effect of cell expansion is proportionally much larger once
P is present. Relative to the $+0\%$ cell, the crystalline fraction drops by
only 2.5--9.5\% for 0P, but by 23.8--32.8\% for 20P and 38.7--44.1\% for 30P.
P and cell expansion thus act synergistically, not independently: P already
frustrates fcc packing, so the extra free volume from expansion has a
proportionally larger disordering effect on a host that is already partially
disordered. The doped leaves sit near the amorphous floor (0.11--0.20), so
the amplification is best read in relative terms.
