\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that a cheap, reference-anchored global-optimization screening
identifies amorphous minima in an interstitial alloy. The method models the
amorphous state as the low-energy basin around a chosen crystalline reference,
enumerates that basin with a surrogate-guided search, and confirms
amorphization with the Steinhardt $q_6$ crystalline fraction. The reference is
a physical choice the user controls --- unique for a host with a single stable
phase, a genuine choice for a polymorphic host.

Applied to interstitial Pt(P), the screening finds that 20--30~at.\% P marks
the onset of structural amorphization: the crystalline fraction collapses from
0.99 in the undoped host to 0.09--0.18, a trend cross-checked by the XRD
integrated crystallinity index. The effect saturates with concentration (the
20P and 30P systems are indistinguishable) and is mildly promoted by cell
expansion, which adds free volume and acts synergistically with the
P-induced frustration of fcc packing. The candidate ensemble is enumerated at
a fraction of the cost of first-principles melt-quench molecular dynamics, and
the surrogate drives the search reliably in the low-energy regime that matters
for selection.

The reference-anchored screening thus offers a practical route to the
structural verification of amorphous alloys, leaving the expensive property
calculation to the few amorphous minima it identifies. Because the structural
claims rest on energetics from a single-DFT-step evaluator, a force-converged
re-relaxation of the P-doped minima is a natural next step before any
force-based property is computed; extending the approach to polymorphic hosts,
where the reference is a genuine choice, is a further direction.
