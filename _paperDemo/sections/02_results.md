## 2.1 The island ground state and the flat basin

For all four models the lowest-energy structure found by the search is an **island** — a
3D metal cluster — rather than a flat film. The flat configuration is a distinct,
higher-energy basin. This is shown in the potential-energy-surface maps of
Fig. 1 (relative energy per atom vs film flatness ΔZ).

| Model | ΔZ at lowest energy (Å) | flat-basin min dE/N (eV/atom) |
|-------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Fe/MgO | 3.65 | 0.1888 |
| Fe-B/MgO | 3.77 | 0.1493 |
| Fe-Co/MgO | 2.75 | 0.1941 |
| Fe-Co-B/MgO | 3.45 | 0.1494 |

In every model the global minimum sits at ΔZ ≈ 2.8–3.8 Å (a clustered island), while the
flat basin (ΔZ ≤ 1.0 Å) lies 0.15–0.19 eV/atom above it. The flat film is therefore a
**metastable-like, higher-energy configuration**, not the ground state — consistent with
the biased search being seeded from a flat reference layer (Methods §1.2).

## 2.2 Boron lowers the flat-state energy

Introducing boron into the film lowers the relative energy of the flat basin in **both**
hosts (Fig. 2):

| Host | without B (eV/atom) | with B (eV/atom) | Δ (eV/atom) |
|------|---------------------|------------------|-------------|
| Fe | 0.1888 | 0.1493 | **−0.040** |
| Fe-Co | 0.1941 | 0.1494 | **−0.045** |

Boron brings the flat wetting state **closer to the ground state** by ~0.04 eV/atom,
regardless of whether the host is pure Fe or Fe-Co. Consistent with this, boron increases
the fraction of flat-basin structures sampled by the search: 0.165 → 0.208 in the Fe host
and 0.214 → 0.242 in the Fe-Co host.

## 2.3 Cobalt has little effect

In contrast, adding cobalt alone barely changes the flat-state energy: 0.1888 → 0.1941
eV/atom (+0.005) without boron, and essentially no change (0.1493 → 0.1494) with boron.
The dominant lever on the flat-film energy is therefore **boron**, not cobalt.

## 2.4 Boron does not bond to the MgO interface

Across the low-energy windowed structures, the fraction of boron atoms in contact with an
oxygen of the MgO surface is ≈ 0 (a single B–O contact in one of 72 windowed Fe-B
structures). Boron therefore does not wet the oxide interface; it remains within the metal
film. This is consistent with the picture in which boron acts on the film's internal
energetics (stabilising the flat configuration) rather than by forming interfacial bonds.

## 2.5 Summary of the main results

- The ground state is an **island** in all four models; the flat film is a higher-energy basin.
- **Boron lowers the flat-state energy by ~0.04 eV/atom in both Fe and Fe-Co hosts** and
  increases flat-basin sampling.
- **Cobalt alone has little effect.**
- **Boron does not bond to the MgO interface.**

The mechanistic origin of these trends (electronic structure, interface registry) and the
robustness of the method to its parameters are presented in the Supplementary Material.
