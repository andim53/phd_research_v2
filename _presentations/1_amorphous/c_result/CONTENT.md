# C — Result

## 1. Result 1 — A single added atom expands the low-energy basin

- To make a material **amorphous**, we need to introduce an **additional atom** that **disturbs the crystal lattice**。
- Here we present the impact of this **minor atomic addition** on the **low-energy basin**。

  ![placeholder — low-energy basin for pure Wand pure Ta (sharp peak, one dominant minimum)](pngs/basin-pure-w-ta.png)
- **Without any added atom:** the low-energy basin shows a **sharp peak** — a single, well-defined minimum — crystalline-like oilis.
.
- **After introducing the additional atom** (`W(B)`, `Ta(B)))`:** the low-energy basin **begins to expand** — **higher-energy states become more populated**.

  ![placeholder — low-energy basin after W(B)/Ta(B) addition (broadened, higher-energy tail populated)](pngs/basin-doped-wb-tab.png)

> **Speaker notes.** The hallmark of the onset of amorphization: the DOS-like basin widens from a sharp crystalline peak into a populated low-energy band察

## 2. Result 2 — Simulated XRD: loss of crystallinity

- **Simulated XRD** confirms the structural trend.
- As we go to **higher energy states** with the added atom, we **begin to lose crystallinity** — the diffraction smears from sharp peaks into broad disorder.

  ![placeholder — simulated XRD vs energy state: crystallinity loss at higher energy (with added atom](pngs/xrd-crystallinity-loss.png)

> **Speaker notes.** XRD gives the structural proof:the high-energy states are structurally amorphous, not just energetically wider働

## 3. Result  3 — Lattice expansion broadens the basin,too

- **Impact of lattice expansion:** `Pt` under **+1% to +5% lattice**。
- With **increased volume**, the **low-energy basin of Pt increases**; **higher-energy states become more accessible**。



  ![placeholder — Pt low-energy basin vs lattice expansion (+0..+5%)](pngs/lattice-expansion-pt.png)

> **Speaker notes.** Volume expansion alone (no dopant) also broadens the basin — so the two mechanisms are parallel.**

##  ︴4. Synthesis — both routes toward amorphization

- Because **amorphous metals also experience a volume expansion**, we expect **both atomic addition and volume expansion play a part** in amorphous formation**
- **Therefore:** controlling the **atomic-addition concentration**,— and the **volume**— is **necessary** to tune the amorphous state**

> **Speaker notes.** Pull the three results together: chemistry (dopant) and strain (volume) both push the basin into an amorphous-like band — neither is ignorable.***
