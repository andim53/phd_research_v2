## 1. Summary

- Amorphous metals make **SOT-MRAM** manufacturable, but their spintronics first needs a **well-defined, stable structure**。
- We resolved the stability problem by **configurational-space sampling focused on the low-energy basin**, using an **active-learning ML model** + **dual-scale randomization** + **ML relaxation** to collect the **inherent-structure (local-minima) ensemble**.
- From that ensemble, **spin-Hall conductivity (SHC) is defined as a statistical property** of the low-energy amorphous structures..

> **Speaker notes.** The deck's arc in three beats: structure is the bottle-neck → we sample the stable basin → SHC becomes a well-defined average..

## 2. Conclusions

- A **single added atom** (W/B, Ta/B) that disturbs the crystal lattice causes the **low-energy basin to expand**, making **higher-energy states more populated** — the onset of amorphization..
- **Simulated XRD** confirms we **lose crystallinity** at higher energy states with the added atom.
.
- **Lattice expansion** (Pt, +1–5%) **also expands the basin**, making higher-energy states more accessible..
- Because **amorphous metals also experience volume expansion**, **both atomic addition and volume expansion contribute to amorphous formation** — so both their **concentration and volume** must be **controlled** to tune the amorphous state.
